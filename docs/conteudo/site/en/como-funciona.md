# How HelloSquad's AI marketing agents work

**You ask in a conversation. The squad organizes, produces and shows you what needs your decision.**

HelloSquad is an AI marketing team for small and medium-sized businesses. Seven AI marketing agents (coordination, Strategy, Brand & Creative, Content, Growth Marketing, CRM and Results) share your brand's memory and work together. This page walks through how a request becomes a delivery, and who decides at each step.

## In short

- You describe what you need in your own words, in a conversation.
- Nexo, the squad's coordinator, turns the request into a piece of work and calls in the right specialists.
- The specialists produce using Brand Memory: what your business has already decided.
- You review, ask for changes or approve. Every version is saved.
- Publishing, sending a message and spending on ads each need their own authorization, separate from approval.

## 1. You say what you need, in your own words

You don't need the technical name for what you want. You can write "I want to sell more on weekends," "I need posts for the week" or "why doesn't my website show up in search?" Your request comes in through the Squad Room, the conversation with every specialist, or straight into one specialist's conversation.

If something essential is missing, the squad asks a few direct questions before it starts. If your request is enough, nobody asks for the sake of asking.

## 2. Nexo organizes the work and calls in the right specialist

Nexo is the squad's coordinator. He identifies the goal, the context and the constraints of your request, then picks the path: a simple request goes to one specialist; a request that spans several specialties becomes a project with steps, owners and recorded decisions.

Every piece of work has one lead. Other specialists join when they add something to the delivery, not to pad it.

## 3. The specialists produce with your brand's memory

Before producing anything, each specialist checks Brand Memory, the record of what your business has already decided: audience, tone of voice, channels, products, words you avoid and corrections you've made. That's why you never have to repeat the brief with each new request.

Anything a specialist notices about your business comes in as a suggestion ("Otto noticed…") and only becomes a rule when you confirm it.

## 4. You review, ask for changes or approve

Each delivery arrives as material you can review: a document, a visual, a calendar, a report. You can approve it, ask for a specific change or turn it down. When you turn it down, you choose between canceling the work and asking the specialist for a new proposal.

Every version stays in the history, with author and date. Nothing gets lost when you ask for a change.

## 5. Publishing, sending and spending need their own authorization

Approving content doesn't authorize publishing it. Approving a media plan doesn't authorize spending. Every action that leaves your business and reaches the outside world (publishing a post, sending a message to customers, launching or changing an ad) needs a specific authorization, with the account connected and, for ads, an approved spending cap.

The account owner decides. Other people on your team can follow everything, but approval and authorization stay with the account owner.

## 6. Íris tracks the results and proposes the next step

Once something goes live, Íris reads the numbers from your connected channels, always with source, period and unit. She compares, explains what changed and tells you when the cause isn't clear. Her recommendation becomes new work for the right specialist.

With no connected data, Íris shows no numbers: she tells you what still needs to be connected.

## Three ways to work with the squad

You can work with the squad in three ways: a one-off delivery, a coordinated project or an ongoing responsibility in Autonomous Mode. What changes is how long the work lasts and how many specialists it involves.

| Mode | When to use it | Fictitious example |
|---|---|---|
| **One-off delivery** | It starts and ends: a visual, an assessment, a report. | Trilha Urbana asks Bia for a marketing assessment. |
| **Coordinated project** | Several specialties, steps and approvals for a bigger goal. | Miolo asks for its year-end campaign: Bia, Kaia, Otto and Kiwi on the same project. |
| **Ongoing responsibility** | Something that repeats, handed off with a frequency and limits, in Autonomous Mode. | Vitta Estética hands Otto its weekly post lineup. |

## What "Needs you" means

"Needs you" is the list of what only you can decide. The squad never takes silence as approval. Decision requests come in six types:

- **Review**: look at material before it moves on.
- **Approve**: say a version is good to go.
- **Authorize**: allow an external action, like publishing, sending or spending.
- **Confirm**: say a piece of information about your brand is right.
- **Reconnect**: renew access to a channel that expired.
- **Reply**: give the squad information it asked for.

## Autonomous Mode

In Autonomous Mode, you hand off a recurring responsibility, like "prepare the week's posts" or "track results every day." Nexo clarifies the scope, the frequency and the limits, and only sets up the routine after you confirm. Every cycle is logged, with what was done and what failed, and you can pause or revoke it whenever you want.

## What HelloSquad doesn't do

HelloSquad handles marketing. It doesn't do sales support, negotiate deals, run ERP, inventory, purchasing or accounting, or manage your whole company. At HelloSquad, CRM means relationship marketing with contacts who opted in, not a sales funnel.

HelloSquad also doesn't promise guaranteed revenue growth, and it never simulates the results of an action that didn't happen.

## Fictitious example: Miolo's year-end campaign

*Fictitious example: the brand and the numbers are illustrative.* Miolo is a sourdough bakery in Pinheiros, São Paulo.

1. Miolo's owner writes in the Squad Room: "I want a Christmas campaign for preorders."
2. Nexo asks just one thing: is the campaign for in-store sales, website preorders or both? She answers "both."
3. Nexo opens the "Year-end campaign" project and calls in Bia, Kaia, Otto and Kiwi.
4. Bia proposes the concept "Christmas Takes Time," with 36 hours of natural fermentation as proof of care, for neighborhood families and for people buying gifts for their teams, in a warm tone with no artificial urgency.
5. Kaia creates the visuals in Miolo's style and Otto writes the copy. Brand Memory records the one word Miolo never uses to describe its bread, and no copy uses it.
6. Kiwi proposes how to promote the campaign, with a spending cap. Nothing is spent until the owner authorizes it.
7. The owner approves the visuals and authorizes publishing. Íris tracks the preorders over the following days and proposes the next step.
